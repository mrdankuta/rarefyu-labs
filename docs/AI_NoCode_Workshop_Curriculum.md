# AI-Powered No-Code Builder Workshop — Detailed Curriculum

## Workshop Overview

This version of the AI Dev Workshop is designed for **non-developers who want to use AI to turn ideas into working products**. Participants may be aspiring founders, product managers, designers, domain experts, or entrepreneurs who do not write code professionally but need to prototype, validate, and pitch AI-enabled ideas quickly.

The workshop runs from **9:00 AM to 12:00 PM, Monday through Friday**, and replaces traditional coding with **AI-assisted no-code/low-code tools, smart prompting, and rapid validation practices**. By the end of the week, every team will have a clickable prototype, a clear product story, and a credible pitch for Demo Day.

**Cohort Structure**
- 30 participants
- 6 teams of 5 members each
- Roles per team rotate daily where possible: Vision/Founder, Product/UX, Operations/Workflows, Growth/Pitch, AI/Prompts Lead

**Assumed Baseline**
- Comfortable using a computer, browser, and common apps (email, docs, spreadsheets)
- No coding experience required
- Curiosity about AI and a willingness to experiment

**Workshop Goal**
By Friday, every participant should be able to:
1. Use AI tools to design, describe, and generate working prototypes without writing code.
2. Connect apps, data, and AI services into simple automated workflows.
3. Validate an idea with real or representative users in under a day.
4. Pitch a prototype clearly to a non-technical and technical audience.

## Day 1 — Foundations: AI as Your Co-Founder

**Theme:** Understand what AI can build for you and learn to steer it with clear instructions.

### Learning Objectives
- Distinguish between AI hype and practical AI tools.
- Identify AI-powered product opportunities in everyday problems.
- Write prompts that get useful, structured, and actionable output.
- Use AI to explain technical concepts in plain language.

### Concept Block (9:15 — 9:45)
1. **What AI can and cannot do for non-technical builders** — 15 min
   - Generative AI: text, images, code, audio, video
   - Automation: connecting apps, routing data, triggering actions
   - Prediction/classification: sorting leads, tagging feedback, scoring opportunities
2. **The non-technical builder's superpower: clear thinking + AI execution**
   - You provide the problem, the user, and the judgment; AI provides the draft
3. **Prompting demo:**
   - Ask AI to explain "API" like you are talking to a 12-year-old
   - Ask AI to generate 10 startup ideas for a given problem
   - Ask AI to create a user interview script

### Guided Lab (9:45 — 10:45)
- Activity: **"Prompt Gym"**
  - Each participant writes 3 prompts:
    1. Explain a tech concept simply
    2. Generate product ideas for a problem they care about
    3. Create a checklist for validating an idea
  - Compare outputs, then rewrite prompts to be more specific.
- Activity: **"AI Translator"**
  - Paste a technical sentence (e.g., from a developer job description or API doc).
  - Use AI to translate it into plain English and list the questions you should ask.

### Team Build (11:00 — 11:40)
- Each team shares the problems they are passionate about.
- Use AI to generate 10 possible solution angles for each problem.
- Vote and narrow down to the top 2-3 ideas.
- Output: Team's shortlist of ideas + the prompt template that produced the best results.

### Deliverables
- Individual: A personal "AI prompt playbook" with 5 reusable prompts
- Team: Shortlist of 2-3 ideas with AI-generated concept descriptions

### Homework
- Talk to one potential user about one of your team's shortlisted ideas.
- Bring notes from the conversation.

## Day 2 — Rapid Prototyping with AI

**Theme:** Turn ideas into clickable, shareable prototypes in hours, not weeks.

### Learning Objectives
- Build a landing page, form, or simple app using AI-generated no-code tools.
- Generate effective copy, visuals, and user flows with AI.
- Compare no-code builders and choose the right one for a given idea.
- Create a low-fidelity wireframe before building.

### Concept Block (9:15 — 9:45)
1. **Prototype vs. product** — 10 min
   - A prototype proves an assumption; a product serves many users
   - Fakes, manual steps, and "wizard of oz" are allowed
2. **Choosing your no-code stack** — 15 min
   - Landing page only → Carrd, Typedream, or Webflow
   - App with database → Bubble, FlutterFlow, or AI builders like Replit Agent / Lovable
   - Workflow automation → Make or Zapier
3. **Demo:** Build a landing page in 10 minutes using an AI-assisted builder
   - Generate copy with AI
   - Generate or select visuals
   - Add a waitlist form
   - Publish it live

### Guided Lab (9:45 — 10:45)
- Activity: **"10-Minute Landing Page"**
  - Pick one of yesterday's ideas.
  - Use an AI-assisted builder or template to create a landing page with:
    - Clear headline
    - Problem statement
    - Solution description
    - Waitlist or contact form
    - Team photo/avatar section
  - Publish and share the link.
- Activity: **"AI Copy Clinic"**
  - Generate 3 headline variations with AI.
  - Test which one your teammates understand fastest.

### Team Build (11:00 — 11:40)
- Choose the team's primary idea.
- Create a landing page or one-page prototype for it.
- Assign roles: who owns copy, visuals, form, and publishing?
- Output: Team landing page is live and shareable.

### Deliverables
- Individual: One published landing page for a personal or team idea
- Team: Live landing page for the chosen project

### Homework
- Share the landing page with 3 people and collect feedback.
- Update the afternoon Lean Canvas with the value proposition.

## Day 3 — AI Workflows, Chatbots, and Automations

**Theme:** Make your prototype smart by connecting AI to real actions and data.

### Learning Objectives
- Build a simple AI chatbot or assistant without code.
- Use automation tools to connect forms, spreadsheets, email, and notifications.
- Understand the basics of AI "agents" and workflows.
- Design a workflow that delivers value to a user automatically.

### Concept Block (9:15 — 9:45)
1. **What is an AI workflow?** — 10 min
   - Trigger → Action → AI step → Output
   - Examples: form submission → AI summary → email alert
2. **No-code AI tools overview** — 20 min
   - Chatbase, Botpress, or Stack AI for chatbots
   - Make / Zapier + OpenAI or Claude for custom workflows
   - Airtable AI or Notion AI for data tasks
   - Voice / transcription tools (Whisper, Otter.ai)
3. **Demo:** Build a form that sends data to a spreadsheet, triggers an AI summary, and emails the result

### Guided Lab (9:45 — 10:45)
- Activity: **"Build a Smart Form"**
  - Create a form in Tally or Typeform.
  - Connect it to a Google Sheet or Airtable.
  - Add an automation that sends a confirmation or summary message via email or Slack.
- Activity: **"5-Minute Chatbot"**
  - Use a no-code chatbot builder to create a simple FAQ bot.
  - Feed it a document (product description, FAQ, or process).
  - Test it with team questions.

### Team Build (11:00 — 11:40)
- Identify the core "magic moment" in your team's product.
  - What input does a user give?
  - What AI or automation happens?
  - What valuable output does the user receive?
- Build one automated workflow or chatbot for that magic moment.
- Output: Working workflow or chatbot connected to the team project.

### Deliverables
- Individual: One working automation or chatbot
- Team: Core AI workflow or chatbot integrated into the project

### Homework
- Run the workflow or chatbot with one real user.
- Note what worked, what confused them, and what to fix.

## Day 4 — From Prototype to Testable MVP

**Theme:** Wire all the pieces together and test your idea with real users.

### Learning Objectives
- Connect landing pages, forms, workflows, and chatbots into one coherent user journey.
- Run lightweight user tests and capture structured feedback.
- Iterate quickly based on feedback.
- Understand responsible AI: transparency, privacy, and bias.

### Concept Block (9:15 — 9:45)
1. **The user journey map** — 15 min
   - Discovery → Signup → Core action → Value delivery → Follow-up
   - Mapping your prototype to each step
2. **User testing for non-technical builders** — 15 min
   - The 5-user rule
   - Task-based testing: "Show me how you would…"
   - What to listen for: confusion, delight, workarounds, missing features
3. **Responsible AI for founders** — 15 min
   - Be honest about what is AI vs. human
   - Handle user data carefully
   - Watch for bias and unfair outcomes
   - Plan for errors and edge cases

### Guided Lab (9:45 — 10:45)
- Activity: **"Wire the Journey"**
  - Connect yesterday's landing page, form/workflow, and chatbot into one smooth path.
  - Add a thank-you page, confirmation message, or result screen.
  - Use Loom to record a 2-minute walkthrough of the prototype.
- Activity: **"Feedback Flywheel"**
  - Create a 5-question user feedback form.
  - Run it with one other team and capture actionable notes.

### Team Build (11:00 — 11:40)
- Complete the first end-to-end version of the prototype.
- Run at least one user test with another team or a mentor.
- Document the top 3 learnings and 3 fixes.
- Output: Working end-to-end prototype + recorded demo + feedback notes.

### Deliverables
- Individual: User feedback notes + suggested fixes
- Team: End-to-end prototype, 2-minute Loom demo, and iteration backlog

### Homework
- Share the prototype with 3-5 potential users outside the bootcamp if possible.
- Prepare a 2-minute demo script.

## Day 5 — Pitch Prep, Storytelling & Demo Day Planning

**Theme:** Get ready to present your AI-powered idea with clarity and confidence.

### Learning Objectives
- Craft a compelling 3-minute pitch: problem, solution, demo, business model, ask.
- Build a simple pitch deck using AI-assisted tools.
- Create a one-page architecture diagram showing how the product works.
- Lock the Saturday hackathon plan and final task assignments.

### Concept Block (9:15 — 9:45)
1. **The founder pitch formula** — 15 min
   - Hook: why this problem matters now
   - Problem: who feels the pain and how much
   - Solution: demo the prototype
   - Model: how it could make money or sustain itself
   - Traction/validation: what users said
   - Team: why you
   - Ask: what you need next
2. **Visual storytelling with AI** — 15 min
   - Use AI to generate slide outlines, images, and speaker notes
   - Tools: Gamma, Canva, Tome
3. **Demo:** Create a 5-slide pitch deck in 10 minutes with AI assistance

### Guided Lab (9:45 — 10:45)
- Activity: **"Pitch Deck Sprint"**
  - Each team creates a 5-7 slide deck:
    1. Title + tagline
    2. Problem
    3. Solution + product screenshot/demo link
    4. How it works (simple diagram)
    5. Target users + validation so far
    6. Business model / sustainability
    7. Team + next ask
  - Use AI for outlines and copy, but human judgment for the final message.
- Activity: **"Architecture Made Simple"**
  - Draw a boxes-and-arrows diagram:
    - User sees: landing page / app
    - User does: form / chat / action
    - Behind the scenes: AI tool, automation, database, notification
    - Outcome: value delivered to user

### Team Build (11:00 — 11:40)
- Run a dry pitch to another team.
- Gather feedback on clarity, credibility, and emotional impact.
- Finalize Saturday task assignments and rehearsal schedule.
- Output: Pitch deck + architecture diagram + Saturday sprint plan.

### Deliverables
- Individual: Speaker notes for their part of the pitch
- Team: Pitch deck, architecture diagram, and Saturday rehearsal/build plan

### Homework
- Practice the pitch individually and as a team.
- Sleep. Saturday is showtime.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

### Collaboration & Teamwork
- Rotate roles so every participant practices founder thinking, product design, operations, and pitching.
- Pair participants during labs: one drives the tool, the other guides the prompt.
- Require daily team standups and short demos.

### Mindset & Identity
- Start each day with a 5-minute reflection: "What did you prove was possible yesterday?"
- Feature women founders and non-technical builders who used no-code and AI to launch.
- Reinforce that "not coding" does not mean "not technical." Clear thinking, user empathy, and execution are technical skills.

### Continuous Improvement
- Two-minute feedback card at the end of each session.
- Adjust Day 4-5 based on where teams are actually stuck.

### Documentation
- Each team maintains a Notion page or shared doc with:
  - Problem statement
  - Solution description
  - Tool stack
  - Prototype links
  - Demo video link
  - Team roles
  - User feedback log

## Facilitator Notes

- **Demystify, don't overwhelm.** Avoid jargon. When technical terms appear, translate them immediately.
- **Show, don't tell.** Every concept should be followed by a live build.
- **Embrace imperfection.** Prototypes can be ugly, manual, or partially faked. The goal is learning and validation.
- **Tool flexibility.** Have 2-3 alternatives for every tool category in case of login issues or paywalls.
- **Link to afternoons.** Tie morning builds to the afternoon's business model, design thinking, and entrepreneurship sessions.
- **Celebrate questions.** Non-technical participants often hold back. Create a safe, question-friendly room.

## Appendix A: Starter Project Ideas for Non-Technical Builders

1. **Local Service Marketplace:** A simple directory where users request quotes and a chatbot matches them to vetted service providers.
2. **Mental Health Check-In:** A daily WhatsApp or SMS bot that asks how users feel and summarizes trends in a dashboard.
3. **Small Business Content Helper:** An app where shop owners enter product details and AI generates social media captions and images.
4. **Job Application Coach:** A form where job seekers paste a job description and their resume; AI gives tailored interview tips.
5. **Community Translation Service:** A chatbot that translates local language phrases and explains cultural context.
6. **Farm-to-Table Alerts:** A signup form and SMS workflow that alerts buyers when fresh produce is available nearby.

Teams may propose their own ideas, but the starter menu reduces decision fatigue.

## Appendix C: Glossary for Non-Technical Participants

- **API:** A way for two apps to talk to each other. You can use it without understanding the code.
- **Automation:** A rule that makes one app do something when something else happens.
- **Chatbot:** A computer program that answers questions in a conversation.
- **Embedding:** A way to store meaning as numbers so AI can compare ideas.
- **LLM:** Large Language Model — the AI behind ChatGPT and Claude that understands and generates text.
- **MVP:** Minimum Viable Product — the smallest version of your idea that proves it can work.
- **No-code:** Building software using visual tools instead of writing code.
- **Prompt:** The instruction you give to an AI to get a useful response.
- **Prototype:** An early model of your product used to test ideas.
- **RAG:** Retrieval-Augmented Generation — AI that answers using your own documents.
- **Workflow:** A series of steps that happen automatically.
